# Keyframes, transcription and the text agents read

Read this before changing `src/keyframes.rs`, `src/transcribe.rs`, `src/hex.rs`, `src/sketch.rs` (`summary.md`) or `src/render.rs` (frames and the contact sheet).

## Images and text

- **Images (`frames/*.png` and `contact-sheet.png`)**: Keep images purely visual. Each keyframe shows the screenshot, active red tldraw stroke(s) for that moment, a subtle click mark (thin translucent ring and small dot, no fill, so the clicked thing stays readable; phone swipes are a thin translucent trail), and a prominent high-contrast `#N M:SS` corner badge (white `#N` keycap + dark `M:SS` pill, top-left by default and dodging to top-right when top-left has ink/clicks). `contact-sheet.png` packs frames in time order (left→right, top→bottom) into aspect-ratio-justified rows separated by tight 6 px gutters on a dark canvas (no card borders or rounded frame masks, capped at 1600 px long edge / ~1.56 MP) with no rasterized transcript headers or caption boxes.
- **Text (`summary.md` & Clipboard)**: Put the transcript, `[#N · M:SS]` keyframe index, and absolute file paths (`Contact sheet:`, `frames/frame-XX.png`, `Video:`, `Bundle:`) in plain text (`NSPasteboardTypeString`) without repeating the transcript multiple times, so `⌘V` pastes cleanly into any terminal (Ghostty / OpenCode TUI) or GUI composer (OpenCode Desktop).

## Transcription (`src/transcribe.rs`, `src/hex.rs`)

Apple SpeechAnalyzer by default (measured word times). `"transcriber": "hex"` + `"hexModel"` in `~/.config/scrawl/config.json` (Settings › Recording, `scrawl hex status`, `--transcriber` on `reprocess`/`import`) uses Hex's local API v2 (discovery file `local-api.json`, bearer token, never logged) only when Hex answers `/health` and has a ready model (`installed && verified`; Scrawl never downloads). Hex gives text only, so each speech island (`audio::speech_islands`, clips merged to ≤ 24) is transcribed on its own and its words are spread over it by length: `source: "hex:<model>"`, `estimated: true`, which `keyframes.rs` reads to skip spoken cues. Any Hex failure falls back to Apple for that recording; Hex absent falls back silently (logged once). Settings reads `hex::cached_status` (a background poller), never the network.

Hex requests use `"hexLanguage"` (Settings › Recording › Hex language), default `en`.
The field accepts a language code or `auto`, trims/lowercases it, and saves on Return/Apply;
blank resets to `en`. Scrawl validates URL-safe code syntax only, leaving supported languages
to Hex. The selection is read once per recording and reused for all speech islands/retries.
It does not inherit Hex's dictation settings. An unsupported code follows the existing
Hex-failure fallback. Models may ignore language hints (e.g. Parakeet v3).

Apple's model (`src/speech_model.rs`, `scrawl-helper speech-model [--install]`) is SpeechTranscriber where the Mac supports it, else DictationTranscriber (VMs and Macs where `SpeechTranscriber.isAvailable` is false), for the user's locale, the nearest supported one, or en-US. Each app reserves it for itself through `AssetInventory` (attributed to `dev.kitlangton.scrawl.helper`) and a fresh Mac doesn't have it, so the app checks and installs it in the background at launch (not when the transcriber is Hex) and retries a failed install; onboarding's Microphone row, Try it and Settings › Recording show the download. `transcribe` never downloads for long: without a model it returns `missing` (`downloading`, `unsupported`, `failed`), the summary gets a `No transcript: …` line (`TranscriptData::missing`), and a retryable bundle gets `.transcript-pending` and is transcribed again in place, `scrawl.json` and `summary.md` rewritten, when the model arrives (`scrawl retranscribe <bundle>` by hand).

## Keyframe selection (`src/keyframes.rs`)

- Only cut keyframes on real visual or input events (`Mark` strokes drawn while holding `⇧`, flags, `Click`/`Drag` inside the capture target or phone taps/swipes, `Laser` points, or settled `SceneChange` transitions)—never cut extra keyframes on speech pauses alone over a static screen.
- The one speech exception is a spoken cue with measured word timings (SpeechAnalyzer; never a Hex transcript, whose times are `estimated`): "now", "here", "look", "watch" snap to a transition that starts within −0.3 s…+1.2 s of the word, else take the frame 150 ms after it. A cue keyframe is dropped unless its image differs (scene diff ≥ 0.03) from every keyframe already chosen, so speech over an unchanged screen still yields one frame. Demonstratives and "see"/"there" are weak (they rank with ordinary scene changes) because they are usually filler.
- Budget (`keyframe_budget`): 6 up to 40 s, then one more per 20 s, at most 12 (each image costs a reading model ~1–1.5k tokens). Ordinary events fill only the first 6; the rest are for marks and coverage.
- Marks are never dropped. Marks drawn on the same screen share one keyframe at the last of them, which draws all their ink (`mark.frame` names it; `markId` is the first): the screen counts as the same while the luma grid around their ink, padded by 8%, changed in under 5% of its cells. A group closes at a click or drag, past 20 s, or past 12 strokes. Groups beyond the budget merge with a neighbour: same-screen pairs with the least ink first, else the most alike screens. Flags stay on their own. No other keyframe cuts into a multi-mark group's span. Auto-crop gives a group's ink to its keyframe's crop alone (`Input::pin_ink`).
- Coverage: after marks, a stretch longer than 100 words or 35 s gets a frame from inside it when one qualifies (a click, laser, settled scene change or novel cue); the lead-in before the first keyframe is its own stretch. Speech over an unchanged screen still yields one frame.
- Text per keyframe: each keyframe owns the words from the start of the clause in progress at its anchor (action start, transition start, or cue word) until the next keyframe's start. Clauses break at clause punctuation, recognizer segment starts, or a ≥1.5 s pause. Words before the first keyframe belong to it.

## Experiment `agentContext` (`src/agent_context.rs`)

Off by default (see Experiments in `AGENTS.md`). With it on, `sketch::summary_markdown` hands each keyframe to `agent_context::keyframe_texts`, which recovers the keyframe's timed words from its `said` and breaks them up by what happened while they were said: marks, clicks, drags, scrolls, shortcuts, and repeats of one item within 3 s folded into `×N` (`pressed ⌘T ×3`, `clicked ×4`), ordinary windows opening and closing (layer 0, by app name; titles never leave `scrawl.json`), app switches by app name, ScrawlKit app events and context changes. The text never gives raw coordinates (the images show where): a place is written only when something names it. A mark is its shape, then what it was drawn on when `Mark.on` names it, then its stroke count when more than one (`✎② 0:19 circled button “Window” (2 strokes)`, `✎ 0:05 marked`, `✎ 0:10 drew an arrow pointing to text “you let go, but it's saved…”`); a click is `clicked`, plus its role and label when known (`clicked button “Area”`); a point is shown only with a label. An unnamed drag shorter than 0.15 of the screen is left out; a longer one is its direction (`dragged right`), a named one names its target, `scrubbed` for a slider, scroll bar, scrubber, timeline or progress bar (`scrubbed slider “Playback”`), and one whose label is text read off the screen (below) says what it went across, since a drag is not always a selection (`dragged right across text “They'd work out most of it…”`). Drags of one thing less than 3 s apart are one line (`dragged back and forth ×2`), and a drag the dev page already reported as a video scrub is left to that report. Speech before the first item stays on the bullet line; each mark, or run of events with no speech between them, starts an indented line followed by the words said until the next one. An item's place in the speech moves back to the start of its clause if that began within 3 s, else forward past the clause's last word if it ends within 1.2 s, so a clause isn't cut in half. Events belong to the keyframe whose stretch they fall in (a stretch starts at the keyframe's first word, first mark, own moment or, for a click keyframe, its click up to 1.5 s before); marks always belong to their own keyframe. Without word times (a segment-only transcript) the speech stays whole and the items follow it.

When a keyframe draws two or more marks they are numbered ①②③ in drawing order (`sketch::marks_on`) in the text and `scrawl.json`, never on the image: frames, contact sheets and exports show only the red ink, so nothing covers the screen. Single-mark keyframes stay unnumbered. `agent_context::mark_entries` / `find_mark` look marks up by those numbers: `scrawl inspect --marks [--json]` lists each mark's keyframe, number, time, shape, what it was drawn on and id (`#2 ✎② 0:09 marked (2 strokes) · m2`), and `scrawl zoom --mark 2 [--frame 2]` (or `--mark m2`) zooms on that mark, centred on its time span (at least `--span`, the whole mark plus half a second either side), cropped like its keyframe, with its whole ink drawn from the moment it starts. A number on several keyframes needs `--frame`; numbers shown as ①② win over single marks.

A scene-change keyframe says `[changed: the whole screen]` when its `autocrop.changes` boxes, merged when they touch, include one covering 90%. Smaller changed regions have nothing to name them yet, so the text leaves them to the image. A keyframe with no speech or items falls back to its own description without positions (`scene changed`).

With `agentContext` on when a Mac capture starts (not the iPhone mirror, and only with Accessibility granted), `crate::ax` looks up what each click and ⇧ stroke lands on in the Accessibility tree: the element at the mouse-down point, and for a stroke the middle of its box (just above a flat stroke, since an underline sits under what it marks). A click on a button's text or icon is named by the button (up to two parents). It reads the role description, a label (title, description, value, placeholder), `AXIdentifier` or `AXDOMIdentifier`, and the file name of the window's `AXDocument`; never a window title, never the value of a secure or other input field, and every string cut to one line of 60 characters. An input field (secure, text, search, combo box) with nothing else to name it is named by its kind alone, in English and unquoted so it never reads as text on the screen (`✎ 0:05 underlined search field`, `dragged secure text field right`), so its typed value isn't read off the screen later either. Lookups run one at a time on the `scrawl-ax` thread with a 0.1 s messaging timeout and a 250 ms budget each; lookups that hit Scrawl's own windows retry on the app whose window is under the point. `finish_session` waits up to 400 ms for running lookups and puts the results on `Click.role` / `Click.label` (`“New Tab” #tab-new in main.rs`), `Drag.label` and `Stroke.on` (`button “Area”`, which `Mark.on` and the mark's description pick up). Results of an earlier capture are dropped (`Prober::begin`).

Many apps (terminals, canvas-drawn and many web apps) answer Accessibility with a bare group, so with `agentContext` on, a mark or drag that nothing named (`Stroke.on` / `Drag.label` still empty after `crate::ax` and `page::apply`, or as recorded in `scrawl.json`) gets the text under it read off the recording (`src/screen_text.rs`, `scrawl-helper read-text`, `helper/ScreenText.swift`). The recording never shows Scrawl's ink, so the text is clean: each mark is read from the frame where its last stroke ends and each drag from the frame where it was let go (a selection still highlighted), cropped around the target, by Vision's accurate recogniser in the user's languages without language correction (screens are full of code and names), one helper run per capture with each moment decoded once and at most 4 recognitions at a time (on their own queue: blocking calls on Swift's cooperative pool hung it at 16). The text is picked by shape: a circle, or a freeform mark whose ink closes on itself, names the lines it wholly encloses (a line its edge runs through is left out), else the words mostly inside it; an open freeform mark the words mostly under its box; an underline the nearest line just above it; an arrow the line at its tip (`Mark.to`, within 1.5 line heights); a pin or tap the line under it; a straight line and a drag the words they cross, in reading order from where they start to where they end, either way round. A target Accessibility named, an input field included, is never read; the region read around any other target is padded (48 pt around a mark, 200 pt either side of a drag), so it can take in a nearby field's pixels, of which only mask dots are filtered out. Lines Vision is unsure of and mask dots are dropped, a region with no area inside the frame is skipped, and the text itself is one line of at most 60 characters. It is stored with a role like an Accessibility label, `text “They'd work out most of it…”`, so the summary never shows it as a bare quote beside the quoted speech (`✎① 0:06 circled text “Hold ⇧ (Shift)…” “You notice…”`); that `text “` prefix is what makes a drag read `dragged right across text “…”`. It runs at finish alongside transcription (`text Nms` in the `finish timings:` line when there was something to read) and in `scrawl reprocess --experiment agentContext`; with the experiment off nothing is read or started. `SCRAWL_TEXT_MOVIE=<recording> cargo test --locked screen_text -- --ignored` checks that one call with 24 regions answers in a few seconds. Not for the iPhone mirror or ScrawlKit imports yet.

Dev pages that load `/sdk.js` add what only the page knows (`src/page.rs`, `src/sdk.js`). With `agentContext` on, `/web/register` answers `"context": true` to loopback pages, and the script then hooks in once: the element under each `pointerdown` and under ⇧ mouse moves (every 250 ms) as tag, id, two classes, a label (`aria-label`, `title`, `alt`, `data-testid`, else up to 60 characters of text; for form fields only the placeholder or name, nothing for password fields, never a typed value), and the component and source `file:line` from React's fiber (`_debugSource`, or the first project frame of `_debugStack`) or a locator attribute (`data-source-loc`, `data-inspector-relative-path`/`-line`, `data-sentry-component`/`-source-file`); `<video>`/`<audio>` seeks, plays and pauses (file name, time, duration); `console.error`, uncaught errors and unhandled rejections (message and first project frame); and fetch/XHR requests that fail or answer 400+ (method, path without query, status, duration; no bodies, headers or cookies). It batches them to `POST /web/context`, which takes loopback pages from this Mac only, token or not, and drops them when the experiment is off. The app keeps 3 minutes per tab (300 events, 16 tabs) and cuts every string again. When a capture finishes (`finish_session`, not the iPhone mirror), `page::apply` takes the window's events from tabs that were clicked, marked or focused then: a page click within 400 ms of a Mac click or drag replaces its label (`<button.tab-new> “New tab” in BrowserHeader (src/BrowserHeader.tsx:88)`), the most common element under a stroke's ⇧ moves becomes its `on`, and errors, failed requests and video playback become app events (`console error: …`, `GET /api/tabs → 404`, `video clips.mp4 scrubbed to 0:04 / 0:11`, a scrub's seeks folded into its last). The agentContext summary shows a Mac capture's app events without the `app:` prefix.

## Experiment `liveTranscribe` (`src/live_transcribe.rs`, `helper/LiveTranscribe.swift`)

Off by default (see Experiments in `AGENTS.md`). With it on and Apple Speech the transcriber, `AudioRecorder::start` also starts `scrawl-helper transcribe-stream` (one SpeechAnalyzer, finalized results only; it never downloads a model, so one that isn't installed fails at once) and a thread that feeds it the recorder's 16 kHz buffer as `audio::pcm16` every 160 ms. `RecordedAudio.live` carries it to `transcribe::transcribe`, which sends the samples it hasn't had from the finished WAV, closes its input and waits up to 6 s for the tail; the helper must report exactly the WAV's sample count. Word times are therefore the WAV's own, and match one SpeechAnalyzer pass over `audio.wav` word for word and millisecond for millisecond (the batch path splits recordings over 22 s at silences and transcribes the pieces in parallel, so long recordings can differ from it at the cuts). Any failure is logged (`Live transcription failed, …`) and the WAV is transcribed as before; Hex, iPhone and imported recordings never stream. `scrawl reprocess B --out O --bench 1 --live-speed 1` replays a bundle's audio into it in real time and logs `Live transcription: N words, … tail N ms` and the finish timings.

To compare a summary experiment on real captures, `scripts/agent-eval.sh [--experiment NAME] [--out DIR] [bundle…]` reprocesses each bundle with and without it (Kit's own `experiments` removed from the baseline's config) and writes `before.md`, `after.md`, both contact sheets and frames per bundle, and an `index.md`, by default to `~/code/home/artifacts/scrawl-context/`. Both summaries come from the baseline run's transcript. What a capture recorded live (Accessibility lookups, page events) is kept as recorded, so those only show on captures made with the experiment on.
